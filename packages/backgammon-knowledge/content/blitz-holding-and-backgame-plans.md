Blitz plan: hit and keep initiative before the opponent can anchor or escape. This usually needs board strength and enough attacking material to continue pressure.

Holding game: keep one anchor, preserve contact, and wait for a high-value shot. It is often quieter than blitzing and depends on patience plus racing awareness.

Back game: two anchors with comeback potential, but high timing cost. It is often overused by improving players. Without front-position discipline, a back game can collapse into a losing crunch.

Heuristics like zone-checker counts can help quick evaluation, but they are not absolute laws. Use them with board strength, timing, and return-shot risk.
