Pip count is information, not a plan by itself. In pure races, lower pip count is usually better. In contact games, race lead can be less important than board strength, anchor value, or timing.

Use race margin, not only raw total, for practical over-the-board decisions. Then ask what that margin means here: does it support running, escaping, attacking, or waiting?

Shot math should stay practical for most learners. Counting direct and indirect shots, and comparing likely entering numbers, is often enough to explain why one move changes risk more than another.

A useful coaching pattern is: race status first, then contact reality, then structure tradeoff. This prevents overusing "I am ahead, so I should always play safe" as a universal rule.
